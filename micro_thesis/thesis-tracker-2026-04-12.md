**Margin detail:** Management attributed 5-6pp of compression to free shipping (Brazil), 1P commerce, cross-border trade, and credit card expansion. Excluding a $99M Brazilian tax credit, Q4 op margin was 9.0%. FY op margin ~11.1%.

**Break condition assessment:** The >400bps compression technically doesn't trip because management articulated a clear reinvestment rationale. ### Adversarial stress test
**Bear reading:** The "deliberate investment" framing may mask structurally lower-margin categories. 1P commerce, subsidized shipping, and credit cards are inherently lower-return businesses than the marketplace flywheel. If MELI is buying GMV growth with margin, the compounding thesis weakens — you get revenue growth without earnings power expansion. Credit card portfolio expansion also imports NPL risk at scale.

**Counter:** MELI has a multi-cycle track record of investing ahead and harvesting. Record NPS across all three core geos. GMV items sold +43% suggests real engagement, not bought volume. Brazil 1P is a category-expansion play (groceries, electronics) that deepens the moat.

**Net:** Reinvestment story is credible but not self-evident. Must see margin stabilization or inflection by Q2 2026. If op margin stays sub-10% for two more quarters without accelerating unit economics, upgrade to Red.

**Action:** Monitor Q1 2026 margin. Ask: did margin stabilize as 1P and shipping scale? Track credit NPL 90d+ (not disclosed granularly — flag for next earnings).
