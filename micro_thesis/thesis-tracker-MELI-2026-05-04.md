### Operating Margin — distance to break: ~240bps of additional unexplained YoY compression
- **Primary Thesis:** FY25 –160bps YoY OpMgn compression is fully attributable to two named investments: Brazil free-shipping threshold reduction (gross margin driver) and credit-portfolio expansion (provision for doubtful accounts +66% to $3.09bn) [Source: 10-K FY2025, Item 7 MD&A]. Both have demonstrable returns: Brazil unique buyers at all-time records [Source: MELI Q4'25 deck, p.7]; credit portfolio +78% YoY with NIMAL recovering to 23.3% [Source: MELI Q4'25 deck, p.12, p.13]. Reinvestment rationale is intact, so the schema break condition ("contracts >400bps YoY without clear reinvestment rationale") is NOT triggered.
- **Strongest Counter:** Two structural concerns. (1) Q4'25 underlying margin (ex-$99mn tax credit) was ~8.9%, –460bps YoY [Source: MELI Q4'25 deck, p.20 footnote]. If treated as run-rate, that single-quarter print breaches 400bps. (2) The 10-K's new language on Asian entrants suggests the free-shipping investment may be defensive against Shein/Temu-style price aggression rather than purely offensive — meaning it may be persistent, not a one-time reset.
- **Resolution:** The full-year +160bps compression with disclosed rationale keeps the position safe; the Q4-only ex-credit –460bps is a yellow flag that compounds if Q1'26 prints below ~10% margin without a credible new investment narrative. **Net Conviction: M.** Flip-the-verdict observable: Q1'26 reported EBIT margin <8% with no incremental disclosed investment program (e.g., new country expansion, large logistics CapEx wave) would qualify as the unexplained-compression scenario.
- **Sensitivity:** A persistent 200bps incremental compression on FY26 revenue (assume +35% growth → ~$39bn) costs ~$780mn of EBIT. At a 30x multiple that is ~$23bn of equity value, ~9% of a ~$250bn equity market context.
