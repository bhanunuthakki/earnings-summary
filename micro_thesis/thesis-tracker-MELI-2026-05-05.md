## Key Developments This Period

- **Brazil free-shipping threshold cut (Q3 2025) drove GMV re-acceleration and record buyer additions.** MELI deliberately lowered the free-shipping minimum in Brazil in Q3 2025, triggering an all-time quarterly record for unique buyer net additions in the market; unique active buyers rose to 76.8M (+26% YoY) in Q3 and 83.2M (+24% YoY) in Q4, with GMV FXN re-accelerating to +54% and +53% respectively after the Q2 2025 trough of +37% FXN. Management frames this as a returns-positive investment in marketplace density, not a deterioration in unit economics. [Source: Presentation Brief, Q3 2025, Management Narrative and Strategic Initiatives; Presentation Brief, Q4 2025, Highlighted Metrics]

- **NIMAL compressed −580bps YoY to 22.4% for FY 2025, driven by deliberate credit card mix upshift; Q4 2025 QoQ recovery to 23.3% is a nascent positive signal.** Credit card originations rose from ~35% to 42%+ of the credit portfolio mix as management pushed upmarket for better asset quality. NIMAL troughed at 21.0% in Q3 2025 (−310bps YoY) [Source: Presentation Brief, Q3 2025, Highlighted Metrics], recovered to 23.3% in Q4 [Source: Presentation Brief, Q4 2025, Highlighted Metrics], and ended the year at 22.4% vs. 28.2% in FY 2024 [Source: Press Release Summary, FY 2025, §2 Key Business Metrics]. The Q1 2025 brief noted NIMAL had already compressed to 22.7% from 31.5% one year prior [Source: Presentation Brief, Q1 2025, Analyst Watchpoints]. The QoQ recovery direction in Q4 is the primary reason the break condition — while technically triggered on a YoY basis — is not yet treated as a full thesis break.

- **Credit portfolio grew +90.3% YoY to $12.5B; merchant credit penetration reached 33.8% of monthly active sellers.** Allowance for doubtful accounts grew to $3,179M (vs. $1,708M prior year), consistent with the book's rapid scaling rather than signaling a step-change in loss rates by itself. Asset-backed lending (+105% origination) and credit card (+114% origination) were the fastest-growing segments, representing the upmarket mix shift. 33.8% of monthly active sellers now carry merchant credit, up from 24.6% a year earlier and 16.9% two years prior. [Source: Press Release Summary, FY 2025, §2 Key Business Metrics; Presentation Brief, Q4 2025, Strategic Initiatives and Highlighted Metrics]

- **Operating margin compressed −160bps YoY to 11.1% on a full-year basis, with Q4 2025 standalone showing −340bps (EBIT) as the most acute quarter.** Brazil Direct Contribution Margin fell from 20.0% to 13.7% — the most visible geographic margin casualty of free-shipping and logistics investment intensity [Source: Press Release Summary, FY 2025, §2 Segment Revenue]. This is partially offset by Argentina's 41.6% DCM and Mexico's 18.1% DCM. Adjusted FCF recovered to $1.48B for FY 2025 [Source: Press Release Summary, FY 2025, §1 Headline Results], suggesting the investment cycle is self-funding at the group level despite segment-level margin pressure.

- **Mexico emerged as a high-priority growth vector with 53% FXN GMV growth in Q2 2025 and Acquiring TPV FXN growth of 329%.** Mexico GMV FXN was called out explicitly as a strategic acceleration market in Q2 [Source: Presentation Brief, Q2 2025, Strategic Initiatives], and Mexico Acquiring TPV grew >50% FXN through Q4 [Source: Presentation Brief, Q4 2025, Strategic Initiatives]. FY 2025 Mexico revenue reached $6,475M (+38.8% YoY) with 18.1% DCM broadly stable [Source: Press Release Summary, FY 2025, §2 Segment Revenue], positioning Mexico as the clearest incremental growth lever outside Brazil.
