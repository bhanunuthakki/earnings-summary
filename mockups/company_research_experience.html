NU: The Mexico proof is getting closer

The cost advantage remains intact. The next underwriting question is whether Mexico can convert rapid customer and deposit growth into durable local economics without consuming the credit cushion.

01 · Decision

Hold the position. Require an economic proof before adding.

The evidence is improving, but it has not crossed the line that should change portfolio weight. Mexico now has enough funding scale to matter; it does not yet have enough seasoned credit and contribution evidence to underwrite as a second Brazil.

  • Stay at 12–13%while 90+ NPL remains below 5.6% and cost-to-serve remains below $1.20.
  • Add toward 15%only after Mexico reports a second quarter of improving contribution economics alongside controlled losses.
  • Trim toward 9%if loss normalization stalls for two quarters or funding growth requires materially higher deposit pricing.
02 · Thesis

A structural cost advantage is becoming a regional distribution advantage.

Nu began as a low-cost wedge against fee-heavy Brazilian incumbents. The stronger thesis is no longer merely that a digital bank has lower branches and servicing cost. It is that a lower cost base lets Nu offer simpler products, acquire customers organically, price fairly, and compound engagement across a broadening product set.

That loop remains visible in Brazil. Monthly cost-to-serve is approximately $0.90, while active-customer growth and revenue per customer both remain positive.

What is new
Mexico deposits above $3B move the question from demand to economics. The next proof is not another customer count; it is contribution after credit cost.

Why Mexico is different

Brazil’s playbook cannot be copied mechanically. Mexico has lower card penetration, a different bureau environment, and stronger incumbent deposit franchises. Nu’s early customer momentum is encouraging, but loss curves will season after growth has already looked impressive. That makes deposit cost, cohort behavior, and contribution profit more important than top-line account growth.

03 · What changed

The quarter strengthened the scale case and narrowed the credit cushion.

Revenue and net income exceeded the prior-quarter model, led by active-customer growth and monetization. At the same time, 90+ NPL rose to 5.2%. The level still passes the thesis contract, but the distance to the 5.6% warning threshold is smaller.

04 · Valuation

The price offers room for execution, but not for a broken cost advantage.

Base-case fair value is $18.50, about 30% above the current price. The estimate assumes strong but fading customer growth, modest expansion in revenue per active customer, and a gradual normalization of credit losses. The case does not require Mexico to become a second Brazil within the explicit forecast period.

  • Bear · $10.80Losses stay elevated, Mexico contribution remains negative, and mature-market efficiency stalls.
  • Base · $18.50Brazil compounds while Mexico reaches useful scale with controlled funding and credit costs.
  • Bull · $24.20Mexico demonstrates Brazil-like engagement and contribution sooner than expected.
05 · Risks & falsifiers

The thesis should break on economics, not on a narrative change.

The primary risk is that Nu’s low-cost distribution attracts growth faster than its underwriting system can price risk outside Brazil. A second risk is that deposit growth proves rate-sensitive, weakening the funding advantage at precisely the moment credit costs rise.

  • Credit qualityWarning if 90+ NPL exceeds 5.6% for two consecutive quarters.
  • Cost advantageBreak if monthly cost-to-serve exceeds $1.20 without a deliberate service investment.
  • Mexico scaleSupport requires customer scale and improving contribution; customer count alone is insufficient.
