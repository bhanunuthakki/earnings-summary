Eli Lilly (LLY) — Bull / Bear / Valuation Deep-Dive

Senior pharma analyst note · prepared 2026-06-07 · for new-position evaluation

Valuation flag up front: LLY is expensive and just got more so — it ran ~+14% in three weeks to an all-time high (~$1,131) on the Foundayo approval + raised guide. On conventional metrics (~31x fwd P/E, ~74x trailing FCF) it is a premium stock, and base-case 12–24mo upside has compressed to ~+11%. The case for owning it here is a quality-upgrade SWAP for NVO, not "it's cheap." See §4–5.

1. Snapshot

  • Price ~$1,131 (all-time-high close 6/5; 52-wk $624–$1,166). Market cap ~$1.01T, ~894M shares. Dividend yield ~0.6% (payout ~22%).
  • Revenue mix: incretins now dominate. Q1'26 revenue $19.8B (+56% y/y); Mounjaro $8.66B (+125%) + Zepbound $4.1B (+79%) = ~64% of sales. The legacy book (Verzenio/oncology, Taltz/immunology, Trulicity/diabetes, Kisunla/Alzheimer's) is now the minority. FY2025 product revenue $61.0B vs $40.7B in 2024 — the tirzepatide ramp is the whole story.
  • Central debate: a best-in-class incretin franchise plus the deepest obesity pipeline (oral GLP-1 Foundayo, triple-agonist retatrutide) compounding EPS ~15%+ — versus a ~31x forward P/E that already prices dominance, into intensifying price/rebate erosion (MFN/TrumpRx) and a competitive field finally landing oral and monthly entrants.

2. Bull case (what must go right)

  1. Tirzepatide is still inflecting, not maturing. Q1'26 tirzepatide run-rate ~$12.8B/qtr; FY guide was raised to $82–85B revenue / $35.50–37.00 adj EPS (from $80–83B / $33.50–35.00). Volume drives growth even as realized price falls — pricing is the offset, not the engine.
  2. Foundayo (orforglipron) — the oral unlock. FDA-approved April 1, 2026 for obesity (the only take-anytime oral GLP-1), launched post-quarter so it's not yet in any reported number. A scalable small-molecule pill addresses the needle-averse majority and the global supply ceiling injectables can't reach. ATTAIN-1 showed 12.4% weight loss; T2D filings underway.
  3. Pipeline optionality is real. Retatrutide TRIUMPH-1 delivered 28.3% weight loss at 80 wks — best-in-class depth, plus osteoarthritis and T2D data.
  4. Margin leverage. TTM gross margin 83.5%, operating 45.9%, net 35.0%. Consensus net income $25.4B TTM → $31B (2026) → $57B (2030).
  5. Fortress balance sheet funds the build-out. Altman-Z 8.6, interest coverage 37x, D/E 1.4 (debt ~5% of market cap). Heavy capex (~$7.7/sh) is offensive capacity expansion, not distress.

3. Bear case (genuine skeptic)

  1. Pricing is being administratively reset. The Nov-2025 MFN/TrumpRx deal cut injectable GLP-1 DTC to $350/mo (from $1,000–1,350); a Medicare $245/mo weight-mgmt price and a July-2026 $50-copay bridge expand volume but compress margin per script. Foundayo self-pay starts at $149/mo. The "lower realized price" line in Q1 is the leading edge, not a one-off.
  2. IRA negotiation hits in 2027. Semaglutide is in the 2027 negotiated cohort; tirzepatide follows. Medicare-negotiated economics on the core franchise are a structural, multi-year headwind.
  3. Competition is finally arriving. Novo's oral Wegovy passed 2M scripts and still holds ~65% of new-Rx; Amgen MariTide offers monthly dosing (Phase 3). Oral + monthly entrants attack Lilly's two moats (convenience, supply).
  4. TAM/persistence is the unmodeled tail. Discontinuation rates, weight regain, and step-therapy/payer gating could cap durable lives well below bull TAMs. The thesis rests on chronic, multi-year adherence that real-world data hasn't yet proven.
  5. Premium multiple = de-rating risk. At ~31x forward / ~74x P/FCF, any guide-down, label/safety surprise, or pricing headline de-rates a $1T cap hard. FCF conversion is genuinely weak today (FCF/share $15 vs EPS $28) because of the manufacturing build — earnings are real but cash lags.

4. Valuation

  • Multiples: ~31x FY26 / 25.5x FY27 / 22x FY28 consensus EPS; P/S ~12.9x; P/B 28x. Rich vs the market but below LLY's own 5-yr peak (>60x in 2023–24) — the multiple compressed even as the stock rose, because EPS grew faster. Vs NVO: Novo trades ~10x P/E ($43, down from an $81 high) — a broken-growth value name. LLY is expensive-and-winning; NVO is cheap-and-impaired.
  • Reconciling the cached DCFs (do NOT parrot): FMP's models print $713 (levered) and $539 (basic) vs ~$1,005 — i.e. "40–50% overvalued." Ignore them. Both naively capitalize today's depressed FCF ($13.6B), artificially low because Lilly is mid-capex-supercycle reinvesting the incretin windfall. A model that capitalizes trough FCF on a company doubling revenue will always scream "overvalued." Normalize on earnings power: consensus EPS ~$51 (2028), ~$64 (2030); on those, today's price is 22x and 18x — unremarkable for a 15%+ grower.
  • Reverse-DCF / what's priced in: ~$1,131 capitalizes roughly $60–65B normalized 2029–30 net income at a ~16–18x exit — essentially consensus delivered, with modest pricing erosion. The stock is not pricing the retatrutide/Foundayo blue-sky; it is pricing flawless execution of the base ramp. Downside protection is thin if the base case slips.

5. Portfolio fit & the NVO question

The diversification math is attractive in isolation. LLY's ~0.26 correlation to your book is the lowest of any quality candidate (near-zero to software: WIX 0.04, RBRK 0.10, NOW 0.13); marginal-Sharpe +0.43. Healthcare is a true orthogonal sleeve.

But trailing-correlation diversification is a statistical mirage on the dimension that matters. You already hold NVO at 6.8%. LLY+NVO are the two GLP-1 leaders; their ~0.30 trailing daily corr understates shared event risk — both move together on every MFN/TrumpRx/IRA pricing headline, every CMS decision, every class-wide safety or TAM data point. Stacking LLY on NVO creates a ~13%+ single-theme obesity/incretin concentration whose tail correlation in a regulatory/safety shock is far closer to 1.0 than 0.3.

Recommendation: treat LLY as a quality-upgrade SWAP for NVO, not an add. The two have decisively diverged — CagriSema failed its head-to-head (20% vs Zepbound's 24%), Novo guides ~8% lower in 2026, NVO's ~10x P/E reflects a stalling franchise. You currently own the losing side of the exact duopoly. Rotating 6.8% NVO into LLY upgrades franchise quality, pipeline depth, and pricing power without raising aggregate GLP-1 exposure. Fund LLY primarily from the NVO sleeve, then top up modestly to a ~7–9% target (net new ~1–2 pts). LLY's 37% standalone vol argues against going larger despite the seductive Sharpe; size to the theme, not the correlation number. Keep total obesity exposure ≤ ~9%.

6. Key diligence questions & what to watch

  1. Foundayo launch curve (Q2'26, late July) — first reported oral-GLP-1 revenue; scripts, payer coverage, and cannibalization of Zepbound vs net market expansion. The single most important near-term print.
  2. Realized-price trajectory — track gross-to-net and per-script margin under $350 DTC / $245 Medicare.
  3. IRA 2027 semaglutide/tirzepatide negotiation signals.
  4. Competitive reads — Amgen MariTide Phase 3 tolerability, Novo's next move, retatrutide's remaining 2026 readouts.
  5. Capex/FCF inflection — when manufacturing spend peaks and FCF conversion closes the gap to EPS (the lever that validates or breaks the multiple).

7. Bottom line

Lilly is the highest-quality asset in large-cap pharma — a genuine compounder with the best obesity franchise and pipeline — but at ~31x forward / ~74x trough-FCF the easy money is made; the stock now prices flawless execution against a deteriorating pricing backdrop. Base-case 12–24mo total return ~+11% (range −18% to +42%), modestly positive skew. For this book the right action is a SWAP out of NVO into LLY at a ~7–9% weight — upgrading franchise quality without compounding the GLP-1 thematic concentration that a naive 0.26 correlation conceals. If the premium multiple bothers you (reasonable), leg in on pullbacks rather than chasing the all-time high — this is the most valuation-sensitive of the three names.
