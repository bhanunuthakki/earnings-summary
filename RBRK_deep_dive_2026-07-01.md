## 1. Executive verdict

**HOLD the full core. Do not add. Do not put on a LEAP. A ~2–3-point pure-risk trim (10% → ~7.5%) is the only defensible reduction — and it is optional, not urgent.**

The thesis is **intact and, on balance, strengthened** since it was encoded: Q1 FY27 was a clean beat-and-raise, Identity ARR more than doubled to >$50M, the FCF guide was raised +10%, and the "specialist out-grows the scaled incumbent" claim against Cohesity checks out on the numbers. **12 of 13 falsifiers are green**; the lone yellow (growth endurance) is a known, structural watch, not a break.

But two things make this a **hold, not an add, and arguably a candidate for a modest sizing trim**:

1. **There is no margin of safety at $82.** The platform's "$91 fair value / −8.5% undervalued" reading is an artifact of an implausibly low discount rate (WACC 6.88% built on a 0.63 beta) and too few shares (196.5M). Rebuild the *same* forecast at a defensible WACC (9–12%, using the actual ~1.1 beta) and the real share count (~206M, ~224M fully diluted) and fair value falls to **$50–73**. At $82 the stock is at-to-above fair value on any honest DCF, and the Street's ~$95 target is an optionality/low-discount-rate lens, not a value cushion. Adding here fails the 30% MoS bar by a mile.

2. **On a risk-adjusted basis RBRK is already the co-largest single-name bet in the book** — 10% weight but ~65% realized vol makes it ≈2.1× the risk contribution of the 9.6% NOW position and roughly tied with the 13.5% MELI position. By *risk*, not weight, it is oversized.

**Why not a LEAP.** The owner's LEAP-overlay antidote is the right medicine for the *wrong patient here*. It exists to fix "sold winners too early / stayed undersized" on names he **exited or under-owns** (MU, GOOG, NVDA). RBRK is the opposite case: already full-sized, already a top risk line, and *fully valued*. Bolting a far-OTM LEAP onto it doesn't cure a sizing problem — it **doubles down** on a leveraged, high-beta, richly-priced position. Wrong tool.

**Why not a reflexive trim either.** The dominant documented flaw is *selling winners too early* (MU sold ~$295 then 4×'d; GOOG; the TSM monopoly trimmed into strength). RBRK is a working winner with a strengthening thesis; trimming it *because it went up* is the exact mistake. So the trim, if taken, must be justified **only** by risk-sizing — not by price, not by "it's up," not by thesis doubt (there is none).

**The resolution:** Hold the core and let the compounder run. If — and only if — you want to cap the book's single-largest risk contribution, trim ~2–3 points back toward ~7.5% as a mechanical rebalance (mind the tax drag if it's a taxable account). Otherwise do nothing and **bind any future trim to the falsifier scoreboard (growth endurance / NRR moderation), not to the share price.** Re-underwrite at Q2 FY27 (reports ~early September 2026).

## 3. Catalyst-conversion verdict

**The owner's crux: "more AI agents → more data → more backup + security, and RBRK monetizes DATA VOLUME / consumption, not seats." Verdict: EARLY-BUT-REAL — the *mechanism* is confirmed and the *attach* is converting, but the specific agentic uplift is not yet an isolable number.**

**What's confirmed (the differentiator is real).** Rubrik genuinely prices on data volume, not seats — this is the single most important validation of the owner's edge over seat-exposed SaaS (GTLB/KVYO). Two independent primary confirmations:
- CEO Bipul Sinha, Q4 FY26 call (2026-03-12): *"We do not price our product based on number of employees in the company. We price our product based on the size and volume of the data."*
- Rubrik's own licensing guide (primary doc): the core platform (RSC, Scale, Utility, UCL, Cloud Vault) is metered in **terabytes** (front-end TB), and the Utility model is explicitly a *"consumption billing model… capacity-based."*
- **Caveat (adversarial):** it is a **hybrid**, not pure consumption. The Microsoft-365 workload is metered **per-user**, and the current licensing guide says usage is measured "on a per TB *or* per user basis." So "consumption not seat" is *dominant and true for the ~89%-cloud core*, but not universal. The "per-node" idea in some framings is a hardware-appliance descriptor, not a software-license unit.

**What's still narrative (the agentic leg has not hit the P&L).**
- **No metered consumption line, no data-under-management metric.** Across the Q1 FY27 release and full transcript there is no consumption-revenue disclosure, no petabytes, no "data under management" growth figure. Data growth flows into ARR *indirectly* through NRR/expansion (management folds "data growth" into the standard expansion flywheel), so you **cannot isolate the AI-agent-driven portion**. The "AI → data explosion → more billings" tailwind is a reasonable inference, but Rubrik does not assert it as a pricing fact.
- **Rubrik Agent Cloud (Predibase + SAGE governance engine) is pre-revenue.** It is in its *first quarter of selling*; management repeatedly guides "**limited immediate impact**" and **none of the guidance raise was attributed to it**. Early POCs are converting to production (anchor: a US financial-services customer), but there is no ARR figure. It is genuine optionality, not a current engine.

**What IS converting (the attach).** **Identity is the clearest evidence the multi-product / agentic-adjacent thesis is real:** ARR went ~$20M (Q3 FY26) → *(customer count 900 vs 400)* → **>$50M, +38% QoQ (Q1 FY27)** — more than doubling in two quarters, "the fastest-growing product in company history." >50% of M365 bookings now attach identity. This is exactly the "agentic identity sprawl → attach" mechanism showing up as ARR. (Density caveat: Identity ACV is still ~$50–55K/customer — small — so it must keep this pace to become material; see §7 FM2.)

**Bottom line for the owner:** your *differentiated insight is validated* (data-volume pricing, not seats), and the *attach is compounding* (Identity). But the *headline agentic catalyst* (agents → metered data-consumption revenue) is **not yet a number you can point to** — it lives in NRR and in Agent Cloud optionality that management is deliberately keeping out of guidance. Score it **early-but-real, not yet proven in isolation.** Don't upgrade conviction *because of* the agentic story until Agent Cloud carries a disclosed ARR line or Rubrik starts reporting a data-volume metric.

## 5. Unit-economics durability

**The operating-leverage story is real and the standout of the whole thesis.**

- **Contribution-margin inflection (clean and monotonic):** 2.1% (FY25) → **8.0 → 9.4 → 10.3 → 12 → 13.2% LTM** across the last five quarters, guided to **~14% FY27.** This is best-in-class for a 2024-vintage IPO and is the cleanest signal the IPO-era CAC cycle is normalizing.
- **FCF ramp:** −$17M (FY24) → +$22M (FY25) → **+$238M (18% margin, FY26)** → **guide $293–303M (FY27, raised +10%)**. Q1 FY27 FCF $73.6M (19%), more than 2× YoY. The FCF *guide raise* (+10.4%) was far larger than the ARR raise (+1.3%) — the beat-and-raise is **skewing toward cash/profitability over top-line**, consistent with a maturing, higher-quality model.
- **NRR:** ~120% and holding, but **moderating by management's own design** — the wording drift ("over" → "approximately" 120%) and the CFO's pre-announcement are the tell. On a $1.57B base, each 5-pt NRR step-down is ~$78M/yr of net-new headwind (≈¾ of a normal quarter's net adds) — this is the single most important variable (see §7).
- **Net-new sub-ARR cadence & seasonality:** $94M (Q3) → $115M (Q4, all-time record) → $103M (Q1 FY27, Q1 record). Q4 is seasonally the peak; Q1 is seasonally lower, so $103M as a *Q1 record* is a genuine acceleration, not a deceleration.
- **SBC trajectory (the GAAP-margin lever) — verified falling:** 103% of revenue (FY25, IPO-inflated) → **25% (FY26)** → **19% (Q1 FY27).** The thesis's dependency (SBC/sub-revenue must fall) is being met: SBC/sub-revenue ~19.6%, far below the 35% break. **But** the absolute level (~$300M/yr) is still the dominant reason GAAP net loss (−$41.9M Q1) diverges from non-GAAP net income (+$36.2M), and it drives **material dilution**: weighted-avg shares 191.6M → 203.7M (+6.3% YoY); **fully-diluted ~224M** including convert-note effect. This is the core quality-of-earnings knock (see §7).
- **Which tier-1 rule is closest to tripping:** #8 growth endurance (already yellow) and, behind it, #3 NRR (drifting) — both downstream of the **cloud-migration cycle maturing at 89% penetration.** Contribution-margin (#4) and net-new-ARR (#2) are the *furthest* from tripping and improving.

## 6. Valuation — refreshed DCF, sensitivities, and what's priced in

### 6a. The platform's fair value is a low-WACC / low-share artifact
The persisted run (`dcf_runs` id=708, 2026-07-01) reports **fair value $91.09, −8.5% "undervalued."** But it was built on:
- **WACC 6.88%**, derived from **beta 0.628** — indefensibly low for a name that has traded 37→100→44→82 since its 2024 IPO (Black-Scholes expected vol in the 10-K: 56–83%). The FMP profile beta is **1.10**; the owner himself treats it as high-beta.
- **196.5M shares** — the *old* weighted-average; actual outstanding is **205.8M** (Q1 FY27 10-Q) and **fully-diluted ~224M**.
- A generous **6× EV/Sales terminal exit** on year-10 revenue.

The forecast itself is fine — it is ~consensus ($1.6B FY27 → $4.5B by FY36, matching the 21-analyst revenue curve). **The aggression is entirely in the discount rate and share count**, and both flatter the answer.

### 6b. Recompute — same FCF stream, honest inputs
Value per share, holding the DCF's own 10-year FCF and 6×/EV-Sales terminal, flexing WACC × exit × shares:

| WACC ↓ / exit → | 4× (196.5M sh) | 5× | 6× | **6× @ 206M sh** | **5× @ 206M** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **6.9%** (as-run) | $67 | $79 | **$91** ✓ reproduces | $87 | $76 |
| **9%** (β≈1.1) | $57 | $67 | $76 | **$73** | $64 |
| **10%** | $53 | $61 | $70 | $67 | $59 |
| **12%** (thesis default WACC) | $45 | $53 | $60 | **$57** | $50 |

Using **fully-diluted ~224M shares**, subtract roughly another ~$3–5/share from the 206M column → **realistic fair value ≈ $50–68** at a 9–12% WACC.

**Conclusion:** Fair value is **$50–73 on defensible inputs**, **$91 only if you stack the most generous assumption in every cell**. At **$82 there is no margin of safety** — the stock sits at-to-above fair value, and **nowhere near the 30% discount** the owner requires to add (that bar implies an entry around **$35–51** against a $50–73 fair value, i.e., roughly where he actually bought).

### 6c. Reverse-DCF — what $82 is pricing in
To justify today's $82 you must accept **all** of: a ~7–8% discount rate (i.e., treat RBRK as a low-risk compounder), a 6× terminal EV/Sales that *holds* as growth matures, **and** on-consensus revenue delivery to ~$4.5B. Flex any single lever toward normal — a 10–12% WACC, or a 4–5× exit as the growth rate halves — and fair value drops to $50–70. **The market is paying for durable, low-risk compounding with no cushion for disappointment.**

### 6d. Relative check (why it's not a bubble, just full)
- RBRK trades **~9.9× forward EV/Sales, ~11.4× TTM, ~10× EV/ARR** (~8.8× on FY27 year-end ARR).
- Peers (NTM EV/Rev, ~June 2026): CRWD ~18.8×, NET ~27×, DDOG ~11–21×, **PANW ~11.3×**, S ~3.5×, **CVLT ~3.0×**. RBRK is **mid-pack security-SaaS** (below CRWD/NET, ~in line with PANW) and a **3–4× premium to the pure backup peer Commvault**, which the growth gap (39% vs 19%) and NRR justify.
- **So:** on multiples it is *reasonable-for-the-growth*, not egregious. On a cash-flow DCF at a normal discount rate it is *full-to-rich*. Both can be true — high-growth SaaS is priced on forward multiples/optionality, which is effectively a sub-market discount rate. **The honest synthesis: fairly-to-fully valued; a hold, not an add; and not a valuation-driven sell.**
