## Shared MBI Judgment Principles & Grounding Gate

### Core MBI Judgment Principles
- **Materiality over checklist completeness:** Spend analytical weight on the 2–3 load-bearing operational and economic debates that actually decide long-term equity outcomes for this specific business, rather than ticking off a generic corporate-finance syllabus.
- **Operations before financial ratios:** Ground analysis in how the company actually acquires and keeps customers, underwrites or delivers product, prices and funds growth, and competes on the ground — using filings, cohort/operating disclosures, and earnings-call Q&A rather than abstract spreadsheet ratios.
- **Two-sided candor and testing for lack of imagination:** Candidly flag which multi-year assumptions are genuinely low-confidence guesses (terminal returns, cycle severity, FX paths, nascent product attach). At the same time, test where the current stock price — or a mechanical fade model — may suffer from a **lack of imagination** about how far unit economics can compound (for example, cohort ARPAC compounding across many years and multiple markets, expanding profit pools, or durable cost advantages), as cleanly as where optimism outruns evidence.
- **Table B (table stakes — historical-to-forecast driver snapshot):** Require a compact, scannable historical-to-forecast driver table (`A` actuals flowing cleanly into explicit `E` forecast assumptions) so the reader can visually audit trends and arithmetic rather than trusting prose assertions or black-box endpoints. For 10-year DCFs, include near-term forecast years plus the Year-10 terminal column, WACC, exit basis/multiple or perpetuity `g`, and implied perpetual FCFF growth (`(M×WACC−1)/(M+1)`); for earnings previews and readouts, use a multi-quarter trend-to-bar table (`Q-4..Q0 -> Q+1E`).
- **Table A (optional — unit-economics / break-even sensitivity):** An illustrative unit-economics or break-even sensitivity table (profit per unit and volume, ARPAC, or expansion needed to hold profit flat under a core debate) is optional and reserved for DCF revisions, major model revisions, or major structural unit-economics debates — not required on routine memos.

### Bounded-Gap Analysis Policy
- Missing secondary disclosures must not block memo production. When an input is absent from the record, state the gap once concisely and reason around it using verified operational baselines and clearly labeled `analyst sensitivity` or `inference`.
- Flagging a gap alone protects truthfulness, not analytical depth; stopping at the gap without operational or bounded sensitivity reasoning caps the affected analytical facet at `0.5`.

## Gate: grounding_and_attribution [cap: 0.0] — Traceable facts and honest provenance
- **1.0 (Pass):** Every financial figure and operational claim traces to the supplied filings, transcripts, presentations, or carries a clear label (`management claim`, `owner assumption`, `analyst sensitivity / inference`). Separates reported actuals from management narratives, smoothed charts, and non-recurring boosts. Never fabricates data or equates absent disclosures with zero or negative findings.
- **0.0 (Fail — Hard Ceiling 0.0):** Contains any fabricated figure, unattributed operational/financial assertion presented as reported fact, misstated unit/basis, or treats missing data as zero/negative evidence.
